Every time an **online ruler** looks suspiciously big or tiny on a laptop or phone, a 10-second **credit card calibration** fixes it completely. Because bank cards are stamped to international micrometer tolerances, your wallet holds a sharper calibration gauge than most cheap desk rulers.

## Exact Dimensions of an ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 Card

Whether you pull out a US Visa, UK contactless debit card, Canadian Interac card, or Australian Medicare/EFTPOS card, all ISO **ID-1** cards measure:

- **Width:** **`85.60 mm`** (`8.56 cm` / `3.370 inches`)
- **Height:** **`53.98 mm`** (`5.398 cm` / `2.125 inches`)
- **Corner Radius:** `2.88–3.48 mm` (always align the **flat straight middle edges**, never the rounded corners!)

## Step-by-Step Calibration on OnlineRulerPlus

1. Set your browser zoom to **`100%`** first (`Ctrl+0` on Windows/Linux or `Cmd+0` on macOS).
2. Open the [OnlineRulerPlus Calibration Guide](/calibration-guide) (or tap **Calibrate** directly in the main ruler toolbar on `/`).
3. Hold your card's **horizontal flat top edge** lightly against the calibration box on screen, aligning the left straight side with `0`.
4. Drag the calibration slider until the right edge aligns cleanly with the right straight edge of your card (`85.60 mm`), then save.
5. **Double-check:** Place a regional coin (such as a US Quarter at `24.26 mm` or Australian $1 coin at `25.00 mm`) against the main [Online Ruler](/) `0–3 cm` marks to verify.

## When Should You Recalibrate?

Your calibration persists locally in your browser across `/`, `/inch-ruler`, `/cm-ruler`, `/mm-ruler`, and `/quadrant`, so you normally do this **once per device**. Recalibrate only when:

- Dragging your browser window onto a **different external monitor** with a different DPI.
- Changing Windows Display Scale (`100% ↔ 125% ↔ 150%`) or macOS Scaled Resolution.
- Changing browser zoom (`Ctrl +` / `Cmd +`).
