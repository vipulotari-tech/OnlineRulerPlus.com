If you live in the UK, Canada, or Australia—or shop with US brands while owning a metric tape—you routinely translate **centimeters into inches**. Looking at a dual-scale ruler (like the default view on the [Online Ruler homepage](/)) makes the relationship visual instead of abstract.

## The `2.54` Visual Rule on Dual Rulers

On any dual-edge ruler, follow the `1"` tick across to the metric side: it lands four-tenths of the way past `2.5 cm`—at **`2.54 cm`**.

Every time metric moves by roughly **`2.5 cm`**, imperial advances **1 inch**. Over larger spans, keep the neat **`5 cm ≈ 2 inches`** benchmark in mind: `5 cm` (`1.97"`), `10 cm` (`3.94"`), `15 cm` (`5.91"`), and `25 cm` (`9.84"`) all fall just shy of even multiples of `2"`!

## Fast Mental Math Formula (`× 0.4` minus `1.6%`)

When no phone calculator is open:

1. **Quick Estimate:** Multiply centimeters by **`0.4`**. (`12 cm × 0.4 = 4.8 in`; actual: `4.72 in`).
2. **Sharp Correction (optional):** Subtract `0.15 in` for every `25 cm`. For `50 cm`, `50 × 0.4 = 20`, minus `0.3 = 19.7 in` (exact: `19.685 in`).
