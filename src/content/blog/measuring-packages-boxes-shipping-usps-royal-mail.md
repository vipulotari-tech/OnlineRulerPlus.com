Postage surcharges rarely happen because someone misread weight—they happen because an envelope bulged past **`5 mm`** or **`1/4 inch`**, or a padded poly mailer pushed a box past **Length + Girth** cutoffs.

Whether you sell online in the **US, UK, Canada, or Australia**, here is how carriers actually measure cartons and flat mailers—plus quick ways to verify margins with [Photo Ruler](/photo-ruler) or our [Online Ruler](/).

## Official Letter & Large-Letter Cutoff Dimensions

| Carrier & Tier | Max Length × Width | Max Thickness | Max Weight | Common Gotcha |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **USPS First-Class Letter** | `11.5 × 6.125 in` (`292 × 156 mm`) | **`0.25 in`** (`~6.35 mm`) | `3.5 oz` | Non-machinable surcharge if rigid or uneven |
| **Royal Mail Standard Letter** | `24.0 × 16.5 cm` (`240 × 165 mm`) | **`0.5 cm`** (`5 mm`) | `100 g` | Thick greeting card bows > 5 mm inside |
| **Royal Mail Large Letter** | `35.3 × 25.0 cm` (`353 × 250 mm`) | **`2.5 cm`** (`25 mm`) | `750 g` | A4 box mailers often hit 26 mm after tape |
| **Canada Post Standard Lettermail** | `245 × 156 mm` | **`5 mm`** | `50 g` | Anything stiffer than card or >5 mm moves to Oversize |
| **Australia Post Small Letter** | `240 × 130 mm` | **`5 mm`** | `250 g` | Larger than C5/DL bumps immediately to Large Letter |

*Tip:* Always test the **`5 mm` / `1/4"`** thickness limit by laying your sealed envelope flat on a desk and holding our calibrated [MM Ruler](/mm-ruler) or [Inch Ruler](/inch-ruler) vertically beside the thickest bulge (usually over a clasp, coin, or seam).

## How to Measure Carton Dimensions & Length + Girth

1. **Close and tape the box first:** Flattened empty boxes shrink once packed tightly with bubble wrap; always measure the **outer stuffed bulge**.
2. **Assign Length (`L`):** The single longest side (`L`).
3. **Assign Width (`W`) and Height (`H`):** The two perpendicular remaining outer dimensions.
4. **Compute Girth + Combined Size:**
   - `Girth = 2 × (W + H)`
   - `Length + Girth = L + 2W + 2H`
5. **Round conservatively:** Carriers commonly round fractional inches up to the next whole inch (or whole centimeter), so never enter `11.9"` as `11"`.

## Checking Envelope Margins Without a Tape Measure

Need to check an A4 padded envelope or box corner quickly? Lay a standard **credit card (`85.60 mm`)** or **US Letter (`8.5 × 11"`) / A4 (`210 × 297 mm`) sheet** right next to the sealed parcel, snap an overhead photo, and measure exact outer edges in [Photo Ruler](/photo-ruler). For thickness under `25 mm`, verify directly against your calibrated [Online Ruler](/).
