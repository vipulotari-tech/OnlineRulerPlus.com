If you have ever printed a sewing pattern, guitar fret template, ring sizer, or paper ruler only to find a part misses by `4 mm` at the far end, **printer scaling defaults** are almost always to blame. Printers quietly enable *"Fit to Page"* to keep ink off unprintable paper margins—silently turning a `100%` scale ruler into a `95%` toy.

## Exact Print Settings by App and Operating System

Before hitting Print, open **More Settings** or **Show Details** and verify these exact labels:

| Application / OS | Broken Default | Required Setting |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Google Chrome / Microsoft Edge** | `Fit to printable area` / `Default` | Under *More settings > Scale*: choose **`Custom`** -> **`100`** |
| **Adobe Acrobat Reader** | `Fit` or `Shrink oversized pages` | Under *Page Sizing & Handling*: choose **`Actual size`** |
| **macOS Preview** | `Scale to Fit` | Select **`Scale:`** -> type **`100%`** |
| **Safari (macOS)** | Auto-shrink wide web layout | Set scale to **`100%`**; print PDF export rather than raw webpage |

## The 5-Second Post-Print Verification Check

Never trust a print dialog blindly—verify your sheet physically in five seconds:

1. Lay any **ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 credit or debit card** (`85.60 mm` = `3.370"`) along the `0` line of your printed ruler.
2. Check where the right edge lands: it must sit cleanly past `8.5 cm` by `0.6 mm` (or just shy of `3 3/8"` = `3.375"`).
3. If it stops at `8.1–8.3 cm`, **Fit to Page was active**—reprint with explicit `100%` Custom Scale.

## Watch Out for Paper Stretch and Folding

- **Inkjet dampness:** Heavy ink fills slightly swell thin `75–80 gsm` office paper until dry; wait 2 minutes before cutting templates.
- **Laser toner fusion:** High heat inside laser fusers can cause slight `~0.1–0.2%` sheet shrinkage along the feed direction on thin stock; use `100–120 gsm` cardstock for recurring shop patterns.
- **Skip printing completely when near a screen:** For everyday measurements under `30 cm`, save ink and paper by keeping our calibrated [Actual Size Online Ruler](/) or [CM Ruler](/cm-ruler) pinned in a browser bookmark.
